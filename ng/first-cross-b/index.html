4 · Crosses, path A

which parent gives the virgins Every element of path A is on chromosome 2 or on chromosome 3. Thus the direction of each cross is free. Take the virgins from the stock that gives them first. The tables give each cross as one stock against the other stock, and not as virgins against males.

The direction is not free if an element is on the X chromosome. A son takes his X chromosome from his mother, and this work uses males. Thus the mother must carry that element. GJ598, 10XUAS-IVS-myr::GFP at su(Hw)attP8, is on the X chromosome, and only path B uses it. You keep male progeny at the experimental cross. That is a rule for the progeny that you keep. It is not a rule for the parents.

4.3 Stable stocks

You do the experimental cross one time for each arm. A rebuild of a parent costs five weeks. A stable stock removes that cost. A flip is a transfer of the flies into a vial with new food. A stable stock keeps its genotype at each flip, and it needs no new cross.

Chromosome 2 needs no work. ppk23-GAL4 and R56C09-lexA are already homozygous in both parents. Chromosome 3 holds the decision.

RuleReason

Two copies give a different signal. A homozygous stock gives two copies of brp-D3-Cherry and two copies of the fill. Section 7 compares the two arms as a ratio. Both arms must therefore have the same number of copies. Do not compare a homozygous animal with a heterozygous animal.

8 · t-GRASP

t-GRASP splits a fluorescent protein in two halves. The pre half goes in the presynaptic cell. The post half goes in the postsynaptic cell. The two halves join where an active zone comes close to the dendrite. The joined halves make the signal. An antibody stain reads that signal.

See Shearin et al. 2018, Journal of Neuroscience Methods 306:94-102, PMID 29792886. That paper describes t-GRASP and gives the two stocks.

the post halfBDSC 79040, on LexAop PPN1, on the whole dendrite. The carrier is the mouse ICAM-5 ectodomain.

What a punctum shows

A punctum usually shows a dendrite membrane that is close to an active zone. The pre half sits at the active zones of the sensory neuron. A punctum is therefore more than a contact between two membranes.

Shearin et al. 2018 compared the puncta against a separate Brp label. The puncta agreed with that label.

Shearin et al. 2018 also tested nine lamina types against the R1-6 terminals. R1-6 makes synapses onto L1, L2 and L3. t-GRASP found all three. Six other types get no synapse from R1-6. Five of those six gave no signal. Close membranes alone therefore do not make a punctum.

Two known errors. The polarity can be wrong. L4 is the sixth type of that test. L4 makes synapses onto R1-6, and t-GRASP found that contact. A strong driver can also make a punctum at a cell body, where there is no synapse. Shearin et al. write that you must confirm a positive result with a second method.

The limit

A punctum does not identify one synapse. Half of the presynaptic sites on PPN1 lie closer than 294 nm. A confocal without a gel does not separate them. Nobody has compared a t-GRASP count against electron microscopy. Path C gives a yes or no answer about contact. Path C gives no synapse count.

Why this orientation

The orientation follows from the available drivers. ppk25 exists in GAL4 only. The ppk25 arm therefore puts the GAL4 in the sensory neuron. The two arms must use the same reporter arrangement. A difference between the arms is then not a difference of reagent. The result of path C is a ratio. That ratio is the punctum count of the ppk25 arm divided by the punctum count of the ppk23 arm.

That comparison needs one more condition. The two drivers must give the same expression level. The anti-HA channel shows the pre half in each arm. Compare the two levels before you report a ratio.

The sensory driver is therefore GAL4. The target driver is therefore LexA. BDSC 79040 puts the pre half on UAS, thus BDSC 79040 fits these drivers.

The limit of this route is not the chromosome. The target driver must use LexA. Section 3 lists one target driver line with LexA: R56C09-lexA. R56C09-lexA drives PPN1, thus path C reaches PPN1 now. A second target driver line with LexA adds a second target. A split-GAL4 target cannot drive the post half in this orientation.

The gel and t-GRASP

Expansion microscopy is the main method of path A. A gel that denatures by heat probably stops the t-GRASP signal. The heat unfolds the barrel of the fluorescent protein. The two halves then separate. This statement is a prediction from the chemistry. No publication reports a test.

LICONN heats the sample to 95 degrees Celsius in 200 mM SDS for 100 minutes. See Tavakoli et al. 2025, Nature 642:398. LICONN then finds GFP with an antibody after that step. An unfolded barrel therefore does not stop every readout.

The LICONN result does not settle this question. A whole GFP is one protein chain. The two t-GRASP halves are two chains. The Abfinity antibody binds the joined halves only. Test the gel with a t-GRASP sample before you plan one experiment with both.

When to use path C

Use path C when path A stops. Path A stops if the label does not express. Path A also stops if the gel fails. Path C adds no separate label stock and no separate fill stock, and it needs no gel. Path C needs an antibody stain. A stain that does not enter the nerve cord stops path C too.

The stain

The antibody decides the signal. Shearin et al. read the t-GRASP signal with one antibody: anti-GFP Tag Abfinity, ThermoFisher, rabbit. This page calls it the Abfinity antibody. Their figure S1 shows that this antibody finds the joined halves. The same antibody does not find the free halves. A general anti-GFP can also bind a free half. Your choice of antibody therefore decides the meaning of the signal.

This readout needs four channels.

ChannelWhat it shows
the Abfinity antibodythe signal
anti-HAthe pre half
anti-ICAM-5the target mask
nc82each presynaptic site

A blank signal channel. A blank signal channel has two causes. The cells make no contact, or one half is absent. The anti-HA channel shows the pre half. The anti-ICAM-5 channel shows the post half. Accept a blank result only when both those channels are positive.

How to compare the two arms. Write the count rule before you image. A punctum must lie inside the anti-ICAM-5 mask. Record the size range and the threshold from the pilot animal. Use the same rule for both arms. Put the two arms in fixative at the same age.

The laboratory holds seven anti-GFP antibodies. No record shows which of them binds the joined halves only. The record holds no Abfinity antibody. An anti-GFP that also binds a free half makes a false signal. Section 8.3 lists the antibodies to order.

Do not put a new transgene at attP2 on the free chromosome 3. A t-GRASP half is already at attP2. Two transgenes at the same site pair with each other. One transgene can then drive the other transgene in trans. This effect is transvection. Use a different position on chromosome 3, or use chromosome 2, or use the X chromosome.

Mellert and Truman 2012, Genetics 191:1129, show transvection between transgenes at one phiC31 site. attP2 is one of those sites. They also show that an active UAS array drives transcription in trans. In BDSC 79040, attP2 holds the LexAop post half. A new UAS transgene at attP2 can therefore drive the post half. A cell outside the target pattern then makes the post half.

8.3 What the nc82 channel adds

Section 8 needs four channels. Four channels need four antibody hosts. The four hosts are rabbit, rat, goat and mouse. The four signals therefore separate. This section gives what the nc82 channel adds.

The nc82 channel does not show every input to PPN1. nc82 marks the endogenous Brp protein in each cell. It therefore marks the presynaptic sites of each input. It marks more than the labelled pair. It does not name the partner cell of a site.

The nc82 channel gives a different number. Count the nc82 puncta that touch the target mask. That count is the upper limit of the input to PPN1. The t-GRASP puncta then give the labelled share of that count.

7 · PPN1 as the calibration

PPN1 does not test the receptor assignment. It tests the method. You measure the two arms, and you compare each count against the number in the connectome. If the counts follow the connectome, the method counts synapses quantitatively in this system. That result is what permits a reader to accept a later measurement on a target that does separate the two groups.

Regionppk25 familyppk23 family, all predicted ratio
PPN1, whole1109827187 0.41
PPN1, wing neuropil570113839 0.41
PPN1, leg neuropil539713348 0.40

The ppk25 cells are a part of the ppk23 cells, because an F cell is ppk23 positive and ppk25 positive. Thus the second column holds every ppk23 cell, and the ratio is the share of the F cells. Nobody has confirmed that every ppk25 cell is also a ppk23 cell.

the result to look for A measured ratio near 0.41 shows that the puncta follow the synapse counts of the connectome. The same value in the wing and in the leg is a second, independent check inside one animal.

A ratio far from 0.41 shows that the method is not quantitative here. Then stop, and use a functional method instead.

The label of path A. UAS-brp-D3-Cherry is a short form of Brp. A GAL4 driver expresses it. It is not the endogenous protein. Nobody has compared it against electron microscopy. Do not accept that difference as a risk. That option gives no number for the calibration.

Add an nc82 channel. The nc82 antibody marks the endogenous Brp protein in every cell. Count the brp-D3-Cherry puncta and the nc82 sites in one animal. The ratio of the two counts is the calibration. This step needs one antibody and no new genetics.

Why path A does not use STaR. Lillvis et al. 2022 compared the nc82 antibody and a STaR tag against electron microscopy. STaR marks the endogenous Brp protein, thus STaR is the better label. They also recommend a postsynaptic marker, because a membrane contact method loses more sites. Path A has no space for STaR.

STaR needs five elements.

  • the sensory GAL4
  • R56C09-lexA
  • the tagged brp transgene
  • a source of FLP
  • a membrane label for PPN1

Chromosome 2 holds the two drivers, thus chromosome 2 is full. BDSC 77138 is on chromosome 2. BDSC 55753 carries the tagged brp transgene at VK00033. BDSC 77139 is also at VK00033. Those two stocks cannot share a chromosome.

A green tag with a green fill gives no result. A V5 tag gives a result. But each V5 stock also makes LexA in the marked cells. Those cells then drive the label of PPN1. Use STaR only after somebody recombines the sensory GAL4 onto the R56C09-lexA chromosome. That step frees one place on chromosome 2.
