· What the PPN1 counts show

PPN1 does not test the receptor assignment. It tests the method. Measure the two arms. Compare each count against the connectome count. A measured ratio near the predicted ratio shows that the method counts synapses here. A reader then accepts a later measurement at a target that separates the two groups.

The Which path tab gives the predicted ratio. It gives one ratio for the whole neuron, one for the wing neuropil and one for the leg neuropil.

A ratio far from the predicted ratio shows that the method is not quantitative here. Then stop, and use a functional method instead.

The absolute counts

The whole input of PPN1 is 33476 connections. The ppk cells give 81 percent of it. About 27000 presynaptic sites are apposed to PPN1. One site gives approximately one connection onto one target cell. Do not divide by six.

Expect about 20000 puncta in the ppk23 arm. That number holds two PPN1 cells, at the 74 percent that the membrane contact method of Lillvis et al. 2022 gives. That method found 501 sites against the 677 sites of the electron microscopy sample. Their best single sample gives 89 percent, thus 24000 puncta. Do not expect the best sample.

The median distance between sites is 294 nm. The person who does the imaging needs this number.

A count of a few thousand puncta shows a failure. The label, the antibody or the segmentation loses most of the sites. The connectome is not wrong. Decide the threshold before you image.

The label of path A. UAS-brp-D3-Cherry is a short form of Brp. A GAL4 driver expresses it. It is not the endogenous protein. Nobody has compared it against electron microscopy. Do not accept that difference as a risk. That option gives no number for the calibration.

Add an nc82 channel. The nc82 antibody marks the endogenous Brp protein in every cell. Count the brp-D3-Cherry puncta and the nc82 sites in one animal. The ratio of the two counts is the calibration. This step needs one antibody and no new genetics.

Why path A does not use STaR. Lillvis et al. 2022 compared the nc82 antibody and a STaR tag against electron microscopy. STaR marks the endogenous Brp protein, thus STaR is the better label. They also recommend a postsynaptic marker, because a membrane contact method loses more sites. Path A has no space for STaR.

STaR needs five elements.

  • the sensory GAL4
  • R56C09-lexA
  • the tagged brp transgene
  • a source of FLP
  • a membrane label for PPN1

Chromosome 2 holds the two drivers, thus chromosome 2 is full. BDSC 77138 is on chromosome 2. BDSC 55753 carries the tagged brp transgene at VK00033. BDSC 77139 is also at VK00033. Those two stocks cannot share a chromosome.

A green tag with a green fill gives no result. A V5 tag gives a result. But each V5 stock also makes LexA in the marked cells. Those cells then drive the label of PPN1. Use STaR only after somebody recombines the sensory GAL4 onto the R56C09-lexA chromosome. That step frees one place on chromosome 2.

Stable stocks

You do the experimental cross one time for each arm. A rebuild of a parent costs five weeks. A stable stock removes that cost. A flip is a transfer of the flies into a vial with new food. A stable stock keeps its genotype at each flip, and it needs no new cross.

Chromosome 2 needs no work. ppk23-GAL4 and R56C09-lexA are already homozygous in both parents. Chromosome 3 holds the decision.

Two routes give a stable chromosome 3.

How to keep a stock stable.

RuleReason
Select Tubby at each flip of a balanced stock. An insertion that lives as a pair pushes out the balancer. The stock then loses its marker, and you cannot see the genotype.
Keep a balanced spare of each homozygous stock. A homozygous stock has no marker. A loss is invisible.
Keep the spare in a second incubator. One incubator failure then does not stop the work.
Test the reporter every three months. The GFP fill has no body marker. Cross a few flies to a LexA driver and look for green.
Do not keep a stock as a heterozygote without a balancer. The element goes away in a few generations.

Two copies give a different signal. A homozygous stock gives two copies of brp-D3-Cherry and two copies of the fill. Section 7 compares the two arms as a ratio. Both arms must therefore have the same number of copies. Do not compare a homozygous animal with a heterozygous animal.

A male cannot be homozygous for the X reporter. A male has one X chromosome. BDSC 32211 therefore always gives one copy. The Gr32a arm and the chromosome 3 Ir52a arms use it. Keep those arms apart from the homozygous arms in a comparison.

· t-GRASP

t-GRASP splits a fluorescent protein in two halves. The pre half goes in the presynaptic cell. The post half goes in the postsynaptic cell. The two halves join where an active zone comes close to the dendrite. The joined halves make the signal. An antibody stain reads that signal.

See Shearin et al. 2018, Journal of Neuroscience Methods 306:94-102, PMID 29792886. That paper describes t-GRASP and gives the two stocks.

What a punctum shows

A punctum usually shows a dendrite membrane that is close to an active zone. The pre half sits at the active zones of the sensory neuron. A punctum is therefore more than a contact between two membranes.

Shearin et al. 2018 compared the puncta against a separate Brp label. The puncta agreed with that label.

Shearin et al. 2018 also tested nine lamina types against the R1-6 terminals. R1-6 makes synapses onto L1, L2 and L3. t-GRASP found all three. Six other types get no synapse from R1-6. Five of those six gave no signal. Close membranes alone therefore do not make a punctum.

Two known errors

The polarity can be wrong. L4 is the sixth type of that test. L4 makes synapses onto R1-6, and t-GRASP found that contact. A strong driver can also make a punctum at a cell body, where there is no synapse. Shearin et al. write that you must confirm a positive result with a second method.

The stain

The antibody decides the signal. Shearin et al. read the t-GRASP signal with one antibody: anti-GFP Tag Abfinity, ThermoFisher, rabbit. This page calls it the Abfinity antibody. Their figure S1 shows that this antibody finds the joined halves. The same antibody does not find the free halves. A general anti-GFP can also bind a free half. Your choice of antibody therefore decides the meaning of the signal.

What a punctum shows

A punctum usually shows a dendrite membrane that is close to an active zone. The pre half sits at the active zones of the sensory neuron. A punctum is therefore more than a contact between two membranes.

Shearin et al. 2018 compared the puncta against a separate Brp label. The puncta agreed with that label.

Shearin et al. 2018 also tested nine lamina types against the R1-6 terminals. R1-6 makes synapses onto L1, L2 and L3. t-GRASP found all three. Six other types get no synapse from R1-6. Five of those six gave no signal. Close membranes alone therefore do not make a punctum.

Two known errors

The polarity can be wrong. L4 is the sixth type of that test. L4 makes synapses onto R1-6, and t-GRASP found that contact. A strong driver can also make a punctum at a cell body, where there is no synapse. Shearin et al. write that you must confirm a positive result with a second method.

The limit

A punctum does not identify one synapse. Half of the presynaptic sites on PPN1 lie closer than 294 nm. A confocal without a gel does not separate them. Nobody has compared a t-GRASP count against electron microscopy. Path C gives a yes or no answer about contact. Path C gives no synapse count.

Why this orientation

The available drivers set the orientation. ppk25 exists in GAL4 only. The ppk25 arm therefore puts the GAL4 in the sensory neuron. Both arms use the same reporter arrangement, thus a difference between the arms is not a difference of reagent. The result is a ratio. Divide the punctum count of the ppk25 arm by the punctum count of the ppk23 arm.

That comparison needs one more condition. The two drivers must give the same expression level. The anti-HA channel shows the pre half in each arm. Compare the two levels before you report a ratio.

The sensory driver is therefore GAL4. The target driver is therefore LexA. BDSC 79040 puts the pre half on UAS, thus BDSC 79040 fits these drivers.

The gel and t-GRASP

Path C reads its signal on a confocal, thus path C needs no gel. A gel that denatures by heat probably stops the t-GRASP signal. The heat unfolds the barrel of the fluorescent protein, and the two halves then separate. No publication reports a test. Test one t-GRASP sample in a gel before you combine path C and the gel.
