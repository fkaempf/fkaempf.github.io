Receptor types of contact chemosensory neurons

A test to find the receptor type of each sensory neuron type. Male CNS connectome, male-cns:v1.0.

Working page · Florian Kämpf · 11 August 2026 · Related pages: AN05B102, AN05B023b/c, AN09B017.

1 · The problem

A collaborator gave a receptor type to each sensory neuron type. He used the downstream partners of these neurons to do this. But the paper makes a claim about the same downstream partners. This is a loop.

You cannot use the downstream partners two times. You cannot use them first to find the receptor type, and then again to show what the receptor type does. More connectome analysis does not break this loop. Only a measurement outside the connectome breaks it.

2 · PPN1 is the first anchor

A neuron near the middle is the most useful anchor. The goal is to measure what split of ppk23 and ppk25 input each ascending neuron receives. A scale needs points along its whole length. A neuron at 41 percent fixes the middle. AN05B023b at 8 percent and AN05B023c at 93 percent fix the two ends.

PPN1 is also the only target with a driver line that is confirmed and in the laboratory. That line is R56C09-lexA, BDSC 53584. Thus PPN1 is both the best placed anchor and the only one you can build today. Start here.

The two groups mix along the whole dendrite of PPN1. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov D value between the positions of the two groups is 0.02 to 0.06 on the three axes, and 0.08 at most on any principal axis. Thus you cannot separate the groups by position. You must separate them by driver, which is what this test does.

One sentence in the paper must change. The paper says that PPN1 gives evidence for the receptor type of WG3, and cites the physiology of Kallman et al. 2015. The connectome shows that PPN1 takes almost equal input from the two groups. Thus PPN1 does not select one group, and it is not evidence for a receptor type. That does not reduce its value here. A neuron that mixes the two groups is a poor marker and a good anchor.

FlyBase gives the name PPN1 to AN05B023 in FBbt:00111517. This is not correct. The connectome gives the name "Kallman 2015: PPN1" to AN05B102a. Also, AN05B102a is cholinergic, but AN05B023a to AN05B023d are GABAergic.

3 · The method

Do not defend the receptor types. Calculate them. The connectome gives the equations. A GRASP test gives the measurements.

The method uses one fact about the two drivers:

  • The ppk25 cells are a part of the ppk23 cells.
  • Thus the ppk23 driver shows both groups.
  • The ppk25 driver shows only one group.
  • The ratio of the two puncta counts gives R.

An unknown factor k changes the puncta count. The factor k is different for each driver. The factor k also changes with the target neuron, the neuropil and the dendrite. Thus you cannot compare two different target neurons directly.

4 · What the test can show

The test cannot give you all eight receptor types one by one.

You can write one equation for each target neuron. With eight target neurons you get eight equations. Eight equations and eight unknown receptor types look sufficient. The mathematics agrees: the equations are independent, and the condition number is 2.5, which is good.

But each equation has two more unknown values in it. The first is the factor k. The second tells you how the puncta count changes when the number of synapses is very high. You do not know either value. Thus you have eight equations and ten unknown values. Eight equations cannot give ten answers.

Therefore ask a smaller question. Ask if the wing types are correct, and ask if the leg types are correct. Two questions need only two measurements.

There are four possible answers, not two

Nobody thinks that all the receptor types are wrong. The important errors are in the wing to leg step.

Use one pair of neurons and two neuropils

Use one pair of target neurons. Measure each animal two times. Measure first in the neuropil Ov. Then measure in the neuropil LegNp T1 to T3. This gives two values, W and L. Section 5 gives the pairs that you can use. The numbers below are for the pair AN05B023b and AN05B023c.

W = [log Y(ppk25,c,Ov) − log Y(ppk23,c,Ov)] − [log Y(ppk25,b,Ov) − log Y(ppk23,b,Ov)]
L = [log Y(ppk25,c,Leg) − log Y(ppk23,c,Leg)] − [log Y(ppk25,b,Leg) − log Y(ppk23,b,Leg)]

Each value uses one neuropil, the same sensory neurons and the same two drivers. Thus the factor k goes away fully. Do not compare W with L.

ValueIf correctIf wrongShows
W+1.04−1.06 the wing types
L+1.07−1.15 the leg types

The values are log10 units. The signs of W and L give one of the four answers.

Do this first. The equation for R is correct only if the two drivers show exactly the eight sensory neuron types. But 417 sensory neurons in five types have no receptor type. Also, four of the eight types have no molecular evidence.

Find which neurons the drivers show. Use HCR-FISH or MCFO. This work costs less than the GRASP test. If the ppk23 driver shows more than 1.3 times or less than 0.7 times the correct number of cells, stop. Do not build the GRASP flies.

Make the ppk23-LexA stock clean

The stock is ;ppk23-LexA/CyO; 22B10-Gal4/TM3, Ser. Remove the 22B10-GAL4 transgene.

  • Cross the stock to a stock with two balancers.
  • Select the flies that have Ser. These flies have TM3,Ser and not 22B10-GAL4.
  • Also select the flies that have CyO but not Sp. These flies have ppk23-LexA.
  • Cross the brothers and sisters. Keep the new stock.

Ser is dominant. Thus you can do this step with your eyes. You do not need PCR. Keep the first stock. Do not change it.

Make the ppk25-GAL4 stock clean

The stock BDSC 93028 has one insert on chromosome 2 and one insert on chromosome 3. Remove the insert on chromosome 3. There is a second reason to do this. Two inserts make a stronger driver than one insert. The ppk23 stock has one insert. The test compares these two drivers. Thus the two stocks must have the same number of inserts.

Warning. The two inserts have the same marker. Thus you must use PCR to show that you removed the insert on chromosome 3.

The two systems have opposite polarity. Do not mix the halves. t-GRASP puts spGFP11 on the pre half. syb-GRASP and CD4-GRASP put spGFP1-10 on the pre half.

t-GRASP puts the pre half at the active zone. It puts the post half on mouse ICAM-5. ICAM-5 is a dendritic adhesion molecule, and it fills the full dendrite. Thus t-GRASP compares an active zone with a full dendrite. It does not compare an active zone with one postsynaptic site.

t-GRASP gives false signals. In the control of Shearin et al. 2018 the false signal fell to the limit of detection. It did not fall to zero. The same paper gives two false positives. The authors write that t-GRASP is not infallible, and that a positive result needs a second method. Plan a second method from the start.

Expansion microscopy

A confocal joins puncta that sit close together. This is a real problem here. On AN05B023c the median distance to the nearest synapse is 0.37 to 0.41 µm. A confocal resolves 0.25 µm across and 0.70 µm along the axis. Thus 35 to 38 percent of the synapses sit closer than the lateral resolution. And 71 to 74 percent sit closer than the axial resolution.

The overlap between LgLG1a and LgLG1b is not the reason to expand. There is one spGFP pair, so the two arms are two different animals. You never separate an LgLG1a punctum from an LgLG1b punctum in one tissue. The reason to expand is the distance between puncta inside one arm.

Nobody has imaged GRASP puncta after expansion microscopy. This is true for every organism. A literature search gives zero reports, and a colleague who does expansion microscopy confirms this. Read this as untested. Do not read it as tested and failed.

The two halves hold each other by a non-covalent bond. The spGFP11 strand is RDHMVLHEYVNAAGIT, which is 16 residues, and it is the last of the eleven strands of the barrel. The other half holds the colour. The strand closes the barrel and keeps the solvent out. The covalent chemistry happens inside the other half, not between the halves. Thus the gel can pull the two halves apart.

The strand contains no lysine, and the anchor chemistry holds proteins by their amines. But the strand does not carry the anchor. Its carrier protein carries the anchor, and the force travels along the linker. In t-GRASP the carrier is cacophony on one side and mouse ICAM-5 on the other. Aldehyde fixation also reacts with arginine, histidine and tyrosine, and the strand has all three, so fixation probably ties the strand to its neighbours first.

The risk is not only a loss of light. The other half anchors well. If the barrel opens, the strand has nothing to hold it, and it leaves.

Choose the antibody with care. Most anti-GFP antibodies come from full-length GFP. They bind the first ten strands. Thus they also bind the half that has not joined a partner. If you stain with such an antibody, you image where that half is expressed, and not the contacts.

Other groups solved this by choice of reagent. Macpherson et al. 2015 stain GRASP with mouse anti-GFP, Sigma G6539, at 1:100, and they call it anti-GRASP. The same paper reports that chicken anti-GFP, Abcam ab13970, prefers the free spGFP1-10 half. Thus the second antibody is the counter-stain that shows you the expression pattern.

The paper does not claim that Sigma G6539 sees only the joined complex. The practice is established, but the proof is not published. Test it yourself.

Run this control before anything else. Take a line that carries the spGFP1-10 half and no partner. Stain it and image it in the same way. If it gives a signal, the antibody does not report contacts. Macpherson et al. ran this control and did see extra signal with the CD4 form of the half, and much less with the synaptobrevin form.

Macpherson, Zaharieva, Kearney, Alpert, Lin, Turan, Lee & Gallio 2015, Nat Commun 6:10024. 10.1038/ncomms10024

GRASP adds up over time. The bond between the halves is not covalent, but it comes apart very slowly, and the colour forms once and stays. Thus a signal tells you that the two neurites touched at some time while the drivers were on. It does not tell you that they touch now.

Publish this table now. It costs nothing. Give the receptor assignment as an assumption, and give this range as the error bar on each row. This is more useful than one measured number, because it tells the reader which rows depend on which assumption.

Only one target can be built

Both ppk drivers are GAL4. Thus the target driver must be LexA. The laboratory holds one target LexA: R56C09-lexA for PPN1.

SS103018 for AN13B002 does not help. It is a split-GAL4, so it uses the same binary system as the ppk drivers. It gives a target with no sensory driver left to pair with it. The same is true of the four vAB3 lines.

Thus the panel has one point, and this follows from the reagents, not from the choice of targets. A second point needs ppk25 in a second binary system, and that reagent does not exist.

The measurement gets harder under this goal, not easier

The old design compared two targets and took a ratio of ratios, so the unknown detection factor cancelled. A reference point is one number for one target, so nothing cancels. Targets differ in R because they take input from different sensory types, and those same types set the detection factor. A spread of two in that factor reorders the list, because most neighbouring pairs in the list differ by less than 1.5-fold.

Do these two crosses first

  • Cross MCFO into ppk23-GAL4 and into ppk25-GAL4. Register the result to a male VNC template. Then ask two questions. Is every ppk25 cell also a ppk23 cell? And which sensory types does each driver reach?
  • Cross MCFO into R56C09-lexA and into the four vAB3 lines, so that any number you publish later has a known referent.

These crosses need no new transgene. They give every R value at one time, with no detection factor, no background and no false contacts. Nobody has done them.

One assumption under everything has never been checked. R is a share only if every ppk25 cell is also a ppk23 cell. The two drivers are different constructs: ppk23-GAL4 uses a 2.695 kb fragment, and ppk25-GAL4 uses a genomic fusion. If the ppk25 set is not inside the ppk23 set, the share is not a share, and the sentence in the paper is wrong rather than imprecise. The first cross above answers this.

What PPN1 can and cannot tell you. PPN1 is the best conditioned target: many synapses, a clean pair of bodies, a middle value, and the only driver. But its R value is almost exactly the sum of WG3 and LgLG1b. It cannot see the other four sensory types at all. Measured to plus or minus 0.20, it rules out 112 of the 256 possible receptor assignments. That is under one bit. Run it to test the method, not to answer the question.

The pages above examine the ppk types. There is a second question, and the connectome separates it more cleanly. WG1 has the receptor type Ir52a and Ir52b on this page. WG2 has no receptor type. Why this arm is easier

  • The question is open. WG2 has no receptor type now. The ppk question asks you to check a call that somebody already made.
