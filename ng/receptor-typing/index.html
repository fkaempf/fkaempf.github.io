Receptor types of contact chemosensory neurons

Reference points for the ppk23 and ppk25 input to identified ascending neurons. Male CNS connectome, male-cns:v1.0.

Working page · Florian Kämpf · 12 August 2026 · Related pages: AN05B102, AN05B023b/c, AN09B017.

1 · The problem

A collaborator gave a receptor type to each sensory neuron type. He built the types from the downstream partners of those neurons. He added neurotransmitter prediction, morphology against GAL4 images, and wing to leg connectivity co-clustering. The paper then makes a claim about the same downstream partners. This is a loop.

You cannot use the downstream partners two times. You cannot use them first to find the receptor type, and then again to show what the receptor type does. More connectome analysis does not break the loop. Only a measurement from outside the connectome breaks it.

The second trap follows from the first. The types were built by connectivity co-clustering BECAUSE the shapes do not separate. So any assay that matches shapes fails at the step it must succeed at. MCFO fails. A colour depth search fails. These assays compare morphology, and morphology is what could not do the job in the first place.

2 · The goal

The goal is NOT to assign receptor types to sensory neuron types. That question stays open.

The goal is to establish empirical reference points. Take a set of ascending neurons and other second order targets. For each one, measure what split of ppk23 input against ppk25 input it receives. A reference point is a number on a neuron, measured outside the connectome. It does not depend on the receptor labels being correct.

The connectome supplies the prediction for each reference point. The dataset is the male CNS connectome, male-cns:v1.0, fetched from neuPrint unthresholded. Two independent methods reproduce the numbers to within 0.00045.

This document covers the argument and the numbers only. The reagents, the measurement methods and the collaboration live in the other two documents.

4 · PPN1

PPN1 is the neuron AN05B102a. The bodies are 11431 and 12286. It is cholinergic. The soma sits in A1.

PPN1 takes 33476 input synapses in total. 27267 of these come from the ppk family, which is 81 percent. 11075 come from the ppk25 positive group, which is 33 percent of the total input. R is 0.406 within the ppk family.

PPN1 is the first anchor, and it is not bad evidence. Its label sensitivity span is the third smallest of the 57 targets, and the second smallest of the ten targets that carry more than 5000 synapses. It carries 27267 ppk synapses. It is a clean bilateral pair, with a per body standard deviation of 0.009. It is the only target with a confirmed driver line.

One sentence in the paper must change. The paper says that PPN1 gives evidence for the receptor type of WG3. It does not. PPN1 takes near equal input from the two groups. A neuron that mixes the two groups cannot select one of them. This does not reduce the value of PPN1 here. A neuron that mixes the two groups is a poor marker and a good anchor.

The two groups also mix along the whole dendrite. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov D value between the positions of the two groups is 0.02 to 0.06 on the three axes. It is 0.08 at most on any principal axis. So you cannot separate the groups by position on PPN1.

FlyBase lists AN05B023 as a synonym of PPN1, in FBbt:00111517. That is wrong. The connectome carries the annotation "Kallman 2015: PPN1" on AN05B102a, and on no AN05B023 body. AN05B102a is cholinergic. AN05B023a to AN05B023d are GABAergic.

10 · Unchecked and unresolved

Each item below weakens a claim that this project could otherwise make. Keep every one of them in the text.

  • The wing and leg split in the analysis code came from presynaptic type identity. It never came from synapse position. Wing and leg synapse positions do separate strongly, with KS D values of 0.748 and 0.815, against 0.08 for LgLG1a versus LgLG1b. But the wing input forms a tight cluster and the leg input spreads around it. So a single plane separates them only 59 to 62 percent of the time. A correct split needs a 3D neuropil mask.
  • The nearest neighbour spacing check subsampled the large sets. So it cannot support any claim about asymmetry between the two arms. The absolute spacing figures stand.
  • The entry nerve is unresolved for most sensory bodies. So any figure restricted to T1 is a lower bound. T1 carries 1291 of the 7923 leg synapses on AN05B023b, and 428 of the 3803 on AN05B023c. Restricting to T1 barely changes R: 0.090 becomes 0.105, and 0.908 becomes 0.928.
  • 417 gustatory receptor neurons, across five types, have no receptor call at all.
  • The assumption that every ppk25 cell is also a ppk23 cell has never been checked. The two driver lines are different construct classes. Any design that divides one group by the other depends on this assumption. This is a statement about the design, and it belongs to the second document, but it limits how a measured R may be read.

Where a statement above is an inference and not a published result, the sentence says so. Where a check has not been done, the sentence says that the check has not been done.

1 · Recommended method

Label real synaptic proteins and read the geometry. Do not use GRASP as the primary assay. GRASP reconstitutes a fluorescent protein between two membranes. It reports apposition. It does not report a synapse. The alternative marks a real synaptic protein in one cell and the membrane of the partner cell, then counts marked sites that touch that membrane. That quantity has been checked against electron microscopy. GRASP has not.

The reference is Lillvis et al. 2022, eLife 11:e81248. They mark BRP with the STaR method in chosen cells, mark the partner membrane, and count BRP sites that touch that membrane. The paper never mentions GRASP, split GFP, trans-Tango or BAcTrace.

The connection level counts undercount. Expect roughly half to three quarters of the true number. The undercount should be systematic, so a comparison between two arms of the same experiment holds. The absolute number will sit below the connectome. Do not treat that gap as a failure.

The method has a demonstrated sensitivity floor. Lillvis et al. resolved 28 percent fewer SAG to pC1 connections in mated females, p = 0.02. Contacts followed functional strength across single animals at Pearson r = 0.88.

1.1 Configuration for this project

  • Drive STaR with a ppk-GAL4 line to tag BRP in the sensory neurons.
  • Mark the PPN1 membrane with R56C09-lexA.
  • Count tagged BRP sites that touch the PPN1 membrane.
  • Repeat the whole experiment with the other ppk driver.

The connectome prediction is 27267 sites for ppk23 and 11075 sites for ppk25, before the undercount. This configuration needs the two binary expression systems that the laboratory already holds. It needs no new reagent class.

ppk25 exists only as GAL4. The two arms compare only if both sensory drivers use the same system, so both must be GAL4. Therefore the target driver must be LexA. A ppk23-LexA does exist in the laboratory, but it has no ppk25 partner, so it cannot form a matched pair. That constraint applies to every design in this document and it limits the panel to one target, PPN1. It follows from the reagents, not from a choice of target.

2 · Expansion chemistry

LLSM is a microscope, the lattice light sheet. It is not a method. The method is the gel chemistry. Choose the chemistry first. The microscope follows.

Proteinase K does not preserve ultrastructure. That is why digestion fell out of use. Heat denaturation preserves it.

Joshua Lillvis no longer uses the 2022 gel chemistry. He now runs insect brains on LICONN. This comes from a conversation in spring 2026, reported by a colleague. It is not from a paper.

Unpublished CSHL Drosophila 2025 abstract: Lillvis, Alqaluoby, Hu, Ravenscroft, Rubin, Saalfeld, Tavakoli and Tillberg. They adapt the dense mammalian methods to Drosophila. They report whole brain volumes, effective lateral resolution 10 nm, on conventional light microscopes, with ultrastructure contrast like electron microscopy plus molecular labels at the same time. They segment single neurons and find organelles, gap junctions and chemical synapses. They apply it to courtship song across Drosophila species. This is a meeting abstract. It is unpublished and the protocol is not available. Do not plan a critical path on it.

2.1 Effect on the driver problem

The one target constraint exists because the target cell needs a driver, and only one target LexA line is available. Dense ultrastructure contrast removes that constraint. If the ultrastructure lets you segment the target neuron directly from the image, you need a driver only on the sensory side. Both ppk drivers exist. Every target in the candidate list then becomes reachable, not only PPN1.

This is the largest single gain available to the project. It is also the least certain, because it depends on an unpublished protocol.

3 · GRASP against ultrastructure

Inference drawn here, not published Heat denaturation unfolds the GFP barrel and releases spGFP11. This is the same reason that MAP, Magnify and U-ExM are ruled out for GRASP. So the chemistry that preserves ultrastructure destroys GRASP, and the chemistry that preserves GRASP destroys ultrastructure. Nobody has tested this. It is a prediction from the chemistry, not a reported result.

The practical consequence is a fork. Take ultrastructure and lose GRASP, or keep GRASP and stay with the older chemistry. The synaptic protein method sits on the ultrastructure side of the fork, because it labels a real protein rather than a reconstituted one. That is a second reason to prefer it.

If group C saturates, all three groups must be read as intensity, not as counts. Decide this before imaging, because intensity reading needs matched laser power, matched gain and matched staining across the three groups.

Warning elav labels PPN1 itself. PPN1 already carries the post half in this design. So the neuron can make signal with itself. Test this before the experiment runs. Check the insertion of the elav line as part of that test.

5 · GRASP

5.1 What it measures

t-GRASP puts spGFP11 on cacophony at the active zone. It puts spGFP1-10 on mouse ICAM-5, which fills the whole dendrite. So it compares an active zone with a whole dendrite. It does not compare an active zone with a postsynaptic site. A punctum is a place where the two labelled membranes came close enough for the halves to meet. It is not a synapse.

5.2 Polarity

syb-GRASP and CD4-GRASP put spGFP1-10 on the pre half. t-GRASP puts spGFP11 on the pre half. The polarity is opposite. The halves are not interchangeable. Mixing halves between systems gives an inverted experiment that still produces signal.

5.3 It adds up over time

The interface between the two halves is not covalent. It releases very slowly. The chromophore matures once. So a signal means the neurites were apposed at some point while the drivers were on. It does not mean they are apposed now.

5.4 False positives and controls

Shearin et al. 2018, J Neurosci Methods 306:94, DOI 10.1016/j.jneumeth.2018.05.014. Their glia control fell to the limit of detection, not to zero. They report two false positives, at md cell bodies and at lamina L4. They write that t-GRASP is not infallible and needs a second method.

Always include a line that carries the spGFP1-10 half alone. It must stay blank. A positive result in any group means nothing until that line is blank in the same session, with the same stain and the same imaging settings.

The source is Macpherson et al. 2015, Nat Commun 6:10024, DOI 10.1038/ncomms10024. The paper does not claim that G6539 sees only the joined complex. The specificity is practice, not published proof. Their control used a CD4:spGFP1-10 line, which gave spurious antennal nerve signal, against the synaptobrevin form, which gave much less. So the carrier protein matters as much as the antibody. The t-GRASP carrier is ICAM-5, which is neither of the two carriers they tested.

5.6 With expansion microscopy

Nobody has imaged GRASP puncta after expansion microscopy, in any organism. A literature search returns zero reports. A colleague who does expansion microscopy confirms it. Anyone who tries this is running the pilot, not the experiment.

The anchoring detail decides the risk. spGFP11 is RDHMVLHEYVNAAGIT, 16 residues, the last of the eleven strands of the barrel. It closes the barrel and keeps solvent out. The covalent chemistry happens inside the other half. The strand contains no lysine. Anchor chemistry holds proteins by their amines, and the strand does not carry the anchor: its carrier protein does, and force reaches the seam along the linker. Aldehyde fixation reacts with arginine, histidine and tyrosine, all present in the strand, so fixation probably ties it down first. The residual risk is that the other half anchors well, so if the barrel opens the strand has nothing holding it and it leaves.

  • Stain with the primary antibody first.
  • Anchor.
  • Gel.
  • Expand.

This order keeps the gel chemistry away from the split GFP.

Warning Gel chemistry destroys cyanine dyes. Do not use Cy3, Cy5 or Alexa Fluor 647. Use Alexa Fluor 488, CF568 or Atto 647N.

The reason to expand is the spacing inside one arm. On AN05B023c, 35 to 38 percent of synapses sit closer than 0.25 micrometres and 71 to 74 percent sit closer than 0.70 micrometres. Confocal resolves 0.25 micrometres laterally and 0.70 micrometres axially. So confocal merging is real and it costs counts. The reason to expand is NOT the overlap between LgLG1a and LgLG1b. There is one spGFP pair, so the two arms are different animals and they are never in the same image.

5.7 Pilot and stop rule

The rule below is a decision made here. It is not a published criterion.

  • Run the spGFP1-10 half only line, group A, group B and group C on a small number of animals, in one session, with one stain and one set of imaging settings.
  • Read the half only line first.
  • Read group C and record whether it saturates.
  • Read groups A and B on the scale that group C forced, counts or intensity.
ObservationDecision
The half only line shows signal above the limit of detectionStop. Nothing downstream is interpretable.
Group C is blank or near blankStop. The reporter, the stain or the target driver failed. A blank denominator makes every ratio undefined.
Group A and group B are equal within animal to animal spreadStop the GRASP route. The assay cannot separate 0.81 from 0.33, so it cannot separate anything smaller.

1 · Chromosome constraint

Chromosome 3 is full. The two halves of the t-GRASP pair both sit on chromosome 3. Thus every other transgene in this design must sit on chromosome 2 or on chromosome X.

Chromosome 2 carries the sensory driver and the target driver. Chromosome 3 carries the two GRASP halves and nothing else. Read the rest of this document against that rule. A stock fails here for one reason only: it puts a transgene on chromosome 3 that is not a GRASP half, or it puts a transgene at attP2, which a GRASP half already holds.

The GRASP reagents on chromosome 3 cost nothing. The GRASP reagents on chromosome 2 are the reagents with a true collision, because chromosome 2 already carries both drivers. This is the opposite of the usual worry, so state it before you plan a cross.

3 · Why only one target

The experiment has one target neuron. That follows from the freezer, not from a choice of target. The chain is short.

  • ppk25 exists only as GAL4, BDSC 93028. So the ppk23 arm must also be GAL4, BDSC 93026, for the two to compare. The ppk23-LexA in the freezer has no ppk25 partner.
  • Thus the driver for the target neuron must be LexA. Two GAL4 lines in one animal label both sets of cells together.
  • R56C09-lexA, BDSC 53584, is the only target driver in the laboratory that is LexA. It drives PPN1.
  • SS103018 and the four vAB3 candidate lines are split-GAL4. A split-GAL4 target takes the GAL4 system, so no sensory driver is left.

Thus the panel has one point, PPN1, until a second reagent arrives. The other documents in this set list further target neurons and rank them. Those targets are not reachable with the current stocks. A second point needs ppk25 in a second binary system, or a LexA driver for a second target.

4 · Stocks to clean

4.1 ppk23-LexA

The stock is ;ppk23-LexA/CyO; 22B10-Gal4/TM3, Ser. The 22B10-GAL4 sits on chromosome 3. It must go, both because chromosome 3 must stay free and because a stray GAL4 drives the UAS half of the GRASP pair.

  • Cross the stock to a stock that carries two balancers.
  • Select the progeny that carry Ser. These flies carry TM3, Ser and not 22B10-GAL4.
  • Select the same flies for CyO without Sp. These flies carry ppk23-LexA.
  • Cross brothers to sisters. Keep the new stock. Keep the first stock unchanged.

Ser is dominant. Thus this cleanup is done by eye. It needs no PCR.

4.2 ppk25-GAL4

BDSC 93028 carries one insert on chromosome 2 and one insert on chromosome 3. Remove the insert on chromosome 3. There are two reasons. The first is the chromosome rule in section 1. The second is copy number: two inserts give a stronger driver than one insert, ppk23-GAL4 has one insert, and the experiment compares the two drivers against each other.

Warning. The two inserts carry the same marker. You cannot follow them by eye. You must use PCR to show that the insert on chromosome 3 is gone. Plan the primers and the genomic DNA preparation before you set up the cross.

Copy number is not the only difference between the two drivers. The two stocks are different construct classes. Nobody has checked whether every ppk25 positive cell is also a ppk23 positive cell. Matching the insert count removes one known difference. It does not remove that one.

5 · The elav line

The design uses a third group of animals, in which elav-GAL4 replaces the ppk driver. That group gives the total input to the target and makes numbers comparable between targets. It needs no second colour and no second reporter.

  • Check the chromosome of the elav-GAL4 line in the laboratory. It is not recorded here. If it sits on chromosome 3, it collides with the GRASP halves, and you need another elav line.
  • Test whether elav labels the target neuron itself. It does. PPN1 already carries the post half in this design, so the neuron can make signal with itself.

Warning. The denominator group saturates. PPN1 takes 33476 input synapses. That much signal on one arbour is a block, not countable puncta. If group C saturates, all three groups must be read as intensity rather than as puncta counts. Decide this before you image, not after.

6 · Absent from stock centres

A stock centre holds only a part of what laboratories build. The ppk23-LexA in this laboratory is the counterexample. FlyBase carries FBtp0158874, M{ppk23-lexA::p65.V}, a 2.6 kb ppk23 promoter driving lexA::p65, from Vernier et al. 2023, and it shows 0 stocks. The reagent is in the freezer anyway. It may be the same reagent as the Dickson stock GJ1472. Check the Janelia identifier DG78 against it.

Thus always ask other laboratories before you build a new construct. Building takes months. An email takes a day.

ppk25-LexA is the reagent that blocks the second point of the panel. No stock centre lists one, and FlyBase has no ppk25 record in LexA, QF or split-GAL4 form. By the rule above, that is a reason to ask, not a conclusion that none exists.

9 · What to order

9.1 Ask first

  • Ask other laboratories for a ppk25-LexA before you build one. Start with the Dickson stocks. The laboratory already holds GJ1472 from that source. Ask Greg.
  • Ask whether GJ1472 and FBtp0158874 are the same construct. Compare the Janelia identifier DG78.
  • Ask about an Ir52b driver outside attP2, and about an Ir52b-LexA. Neither is listed anywhere.
  • Ask Joshua Lillvis, now at Texas A&M, about the current expansion chemistry and about the BRP counting configuration. Mojtaba Tavakoli and Paul Tillberg at Janelia are on the same work, and Barry Dickson was last author on the 2022 paper, which is the same collaboration that holds the fly stocks. This is one group, not two.

9.2 Build

  • ppk25-LexA. This is the single reagent that turns a one point panel into a two point panel. Put it on chromosome 2 or on chromosome X. Do not put it at attP2 and do not put it on chromosome 3, because the GRASP halves are there.
  • If you build it, match the construct class of ppk23-LexA as closely as you can, and give it one insert. Otherwise the two arms differ in the reagent as well as in the receptor.

9.3 Order

  • Mouse anti-GFP, Sigma G6539, at 1:100. Macpherson et al. 2015 use it as the anti-GRASP antibody. The specificity is established practice, not published proof, so test it yourself.
  • Chicken anti-GFP, Abcam ab13970, as the counter-stain. The same paper reports that it prefers the free spGFP1-10 half, so it shows the expression pattern.
  • Alexa Fluor 488, CF568 or Atto 647N secondary dyes. Do not order Cy3, Cy5 or Alexa Fluor 647 for any protocol that includes a gel. Gel chemistry destroys cyanine dyes.
  • A control line that carries the spGFP1-10 half alone and no partner. It must stay blank. Make this line at the same time as the experimental crosses, not afterwards.
  • STaR components, if you take the BRP counting route. That route needs the two binary systems already in the laboratory: the ppk-GAL4 drives the BRP tag, and R56C09-lexA marks the target membrane. The other documents in this set give the method and the expected counts.

9.4 Missing information

  • MCFO the four vAB3 candidate split-GAL4 lines. Nobody knows which AN09B017 member each line labels. The members of that group sit at opposite ends of the target list, so a line that labels the group averages out and reads as a genuine null. That is worse than having no line at all. This is an imaging job on stocks that are already in the laboratory.
  • Record the chromosome of the elav-GAL4 line, as in section 5.
  • Record the insert count of the cleaned ppk25-GAL4 stock, with the PCR result, next to the stock.
