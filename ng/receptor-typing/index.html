Receptor types of contact chemosensory neurons

Reference points for the ppk23 and ppk25 input to identified ascending neurons. Male CNS connectome, male-cns:v1.0.

Working page · Florian Kämpf · 12 August 2026 · Related pages: AN05B102, AN05B023b/c, AN09B017.

1 · The problem

A collaborator assigned a receptor type to each sensory neuron type. He built the receptor types from the downstream partners of those sensory neuron types. He also used the neurotransmitter prediction, the morphology against the GAL4 images, and the wing to leg connectivity co-clustering. The paper then makes a claim about the same downstream partners. The argument uses its own conclusion as the evidence.

You cannot use the downstream partners two times. The first use finds the receptor type. The second use shows what the receptor type does. More connectome analysis does not correct this problem. Only a measurement from outside the connectome corrects it.

A second problem comes from the first problem. The collaborator built the receptor types by connectivity co-clustering, because the shapes do not separate. Therefore any assay that matches shapes fails. MCFO fails. A colour depth search fails. These assays compare the morphology. The morphology does not separate the sensory neuron types.

2 · The goal

This project does not assign the receptor types to the sensory neuron types. That question stays open.

This project establishes empirical reference points. Take a set of ascending neurons and other second order target neurons. For each target neuron, measure the ppk23 input and the ppk25 input. A reference point is a number that you measure on one target neuron outside the connectome. The reference point does not depend on the receptor types being correct.

The connectome gives the prediction for each reference point. We use the male CNS connectome, male-cns:v1.0. We fetched it from neuPrint without a threshold. Two independent methods reproduce the numbers to within 0.00045.

This document covers the argument and the numbers only. The other two documents cover the reagents, the measurement methods and the collaboration.

4 · PPN1

PPN1 is the neuron AN05B102a. The bodies are 11431 and 12286. It is cholinergic. The soma sits in A1.

PPN1 takes 33476 input synapses in total. 27267 of these come from the ppk family, which is 81 percent. 11075 come from the ppk25 positive group, which is 33 percent of the total input. R is 0.406 within the ppk family.

PPN1 is the first reference point, and the evidence for it is good. Its label sensitivity span is the third smallest of the 57 target neurons. It is the second smallest of the ten target neurons that carry more than 5000 synapses. PPN1 carries 27267 ppk synapses. PPN1 is a bilateral pair, with a per body standard deviation of 0.009. PPN1 is the only target neuron with a confirmed driver line.

One sentence in the paper must change. The paper says that PPN1 gives evidence for the receptor type of WG3. PPN1 does not give that evidence. PPN1 takes near equal input from the two groups. A neuron that takes input from both groups cannot select one group. PPN1 keeps its value for this project. A neuron that takes input from both groups is a poor marker. The same neuron is a good reference point.

The two groups also overlap along the whole dendrite. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov D value between the positions of the two groups is 0.02 to 0.06 on the three axes. It reaches 0.08 at most on any principal axis. Therefore you cannot separate the two groups by position on PPN1.

FlyBase lists AN05B023 as a synonym of PPN1, in FBbt:00111517. That entry is wrong. The connectome carries the annotation "Kallman 2015: PPN1" on AN05B102a, and on no AN05B023 body. AN05B102a is cholinergic. AN05B023a to AN05B023d are GABAergic.

10 · Unchecked and unresolved

Each item below weakens a claim of this project. Keep every item in the text.

  • The analysis code splits the wing input from the leg input by the identity of the presynaptic type. The code does not use the synapse position. The wing and leg synapse positions do separate strongly, with KS D values of 0.748 and 0.815. The value for LgLG1a against LgLG1b is 0.08. The wing input forms a tight cluster. The leg input spreads around that cluster. A single plane separates the two sets only 59 to 62 percent of the time. A correct split needs a 3D neuropil mask.
  • The nearest neighbour spacing check subsampled the large sets. Therefore it supports no claim about a difference between the two arms. The absolute spacing figures remain valid.
  • The entry nerve is unknown for most sensory bodies. Therefore any figure that covers T1 only is a lower bound. T1 carries 1291 of the 7923 leg synapses on AN05B023b. T1 carries 428 of the 3803 leg synapses on AN05B023c. A restriction to T1 changes R only a little: 0.090 becomes 0.105, and 0.908 becomes 0.928.
  • 417 gustatory receptor neurons, in five types, have no receptor type.
  • Nobody has checked the assumption that every ppk25 cell is also a ppk23 cell. The two driver lines are different construct classes. Any design that divides one group by the other group depends on this assumption. This statement concerns the design, and the second document covers the design. The assumption also limits how you read a measured R.

1 · Recommended method

Label a real synaptic protein and measure the geometry. Do not use GRASP as the primary assay. GRASP joins two halves of a fluorescent protein between two membranes. GRASP shows that two membranes lie close together. GRASP does not show a synapse. The other method marks a real synaptic protein in one cell. The same method marks the membrane of the partner cell. You then count the marked sites that touch that membrane. Researchers compared that count against electron microscopy. Nobody made that comparison for GRASP.

Lillvis et al. 2022, eLife 11:e81248 describe the method. They mark BRP with the STaR method in the cells that they choose. They mark the membrane of the partner cell. They count the BRP sites that touch that membrane. The paper does not mention GRASP, split GFP, trans-Tango or BAcTrace.

The counts for a single connection are too low. Expect approximately one half to three quarters of the true number. The method gives the same undercount in each group. A comparison between two groups of one experiment therefore stays valid. The measured count stays below the count in the connectome. Do not treat that difference as a failure.

The method has a known limit of sensitivity. Lillvis et al. measured 28 percent fewer SAG to pC1 connections in mated females, p = 0.02. The number of contacts followed the functional strength in single animals, at Pearson r = 0.88.

Proteinase K does not preserve the ultrastructure. Laboratories therefore stopped the use of the digestion step. Heat denaturation preserves the ultrastructure.

Joshua Lillvis no longer uses the 2022 gel chemistry. He now prepares insect brains with LICONN. A colleague reported this from a conversation in spring 2026. No paper reports it.

Unpublished CSHL Drosophila 2025 abstract: Lillvis, Alqaluoby, Hu, Ravenscroft, Rubin, Saalfeld, Tavakoli and Tillberg. They adapt the dense methods for mammalian tissue to Drosophila. They report whole brain volumes on conventional light microscopes. They report an effective lateral resolution of 10 nm. They report an ultrastructure contrast similar to electron microscopy. They record molecular labels at the same time. They segment single neurons. They find organelles, gap junctions and chemical synapses. They apply the method to the courtship song of several Drosophila species. This report is a meeting abstract. The authors did not publish it, and they do not supply the protocol. Do not make the plan of the project depend on this method.

2.1 Effect on the driver problem

The panel holds one target neuron for one reason. The target neuron needs a driver line, and the laboratory holds one LexA driver line for a target neuron. A dense ultrastructure contrast removes this limit. The ultrastructure may let you segment the target neuron directly from the image. You then need a driver line only for the sensory neurons. Both ppk driver lines exist. You can then reach every target neuron in the candidate list.

This change gives the largest single gain to the project. This change is also the least certain gain, because it depends on an unpublished protocol.

3 · GRASP against ultrastructure

Inference drawn here, not published Heat denaturation unfolds the barrel of the GFP protein. The barrel then releases spGFP11. For the same reason, MAP, Magnify and U-ExM do not work with GRASP. A chemistry that preserves the ultrastructure therefore destroys the GRASP signal. A chemistry that preserves the GRASP signal does not preserve the ultrastructure. Nobody has tested this. This statement is a prediction from the chemistry. No publication reports it.

You must therefore choose between two options. The first option gives the ultrastructure and loses the GRASP signal. The second option keeps the GRASP signal and keeps the older chemistry. The synaptic protein method works with the ultrastructure chemistry. This method labels a real protein, so the heat step does not destroy the label. This property is a second reason to prefer the synaptic protein method.

5 · GRASP

5.1 What it measures

t-GRASP puts spGFP11 on cacophony at the active zone. t-GRASP puts spGFP1-10 on mouse ICAM-5, which fills the whole dendrite. t-GRASP therefore compares an active zone with a whole dendrite. t-GRASP does not compare an active zone with a postsynaptic site. A punctum marks a place where the two labelled membranes came close together. The two halves then met at that place. A punctum does not mark a synapse.

5.2 Polarity

syb-GRASP and CD4-GRASP put spGFP1-10 on the pre half. t-GRASP puts spGFP11 on the pre half. The two systems have opposite polarity. You cannot exchange the halves between the two systems. A mixture of halves from two systems gives an inverted experiment. That inverted experiment still gives a signal.

5.3 The signal accumulates with time

The two halves hold together without a covalent bond. The two halves separate very slowly. The chromophore matures one time. A signal therefore shows that the neurites lay close together at some time while the driver lines were active. A signal does not show that the neurites lie close together now.

5.4 False positives and controls

Shearin et al. 2018, J Neurosci Methods 306:94, DOI 10.1016/j.jneumeth.2018.05.014. Their control with glia gave a signal at the limit of detection. That signal did not fall to zero. They report two false positives, at the md cell bodies and at the lamina L4. They write that t-GRASP makes errors and needs a second method.

Always include a line that carries the spGFP1-10 half alone. That line must stay blank. Read that line in the same session, with the same stain and the same imaging settings. Accept a positive result in a group only after that line stays blank.

5.6 With expansion microscopy

Nobody has imaged GRASP puncta after expansion microscopy, in any organism. A search of the literature returns zero reports. A colleague who uses expansion microscopy confirms this. The first person who tries this runs a pilot experiment.

The anchor chemistry decides the risk. spGFP11 has the sequence RDHMVLHEYVNAAGIT, which is 16 residues. spGFP11 forms the last of the eleven strands of the barrel. This strand closes the barrel and keeps the solvent outside. The covalent chemistry acts inside the other half. The strand contains no lysine. The anchor chemistry binds a protein at its amine groups. The strand therefore carries no anchor. The carrier protein of the strand carries the anchor, and the force reaches the junction between the two halves along the linker. Aldehyde fixation reacts with arginine, histidine and tyrosine. The strand contains all three of these residues. The fixation therefore probably holds the strand first. One risk remains. The other half holds its anchor well. The barrel may open, and the strand then has no anchor and leaves the complex.

Warning Gel chemistry destroys cyanine dyes. Do not use Cy3, Cy5 or Alexa Fluor 647. Use Alexa Fluor 488, CF568 or Atto 647N.

  • Stain the sample with the primary antibody first.
  • Anchor the proteins.
  • Make the gel.
  • Expand the gel.

This order keeps the gel chemistry away from the split GFP protein.

Expand the sample because the synapses lie close together within one group. On AN05B023c, 35 to 38 percent of the synapses lie closer together than 0.25 micrometres. On the same neuron, 71 to 74 percent lie closer together than 0.70 micrometres. A confocal microscope resolves 0.25 micrometres in the lateral direction. The same microscope resolves 0.70 micrometres in the axial direction. A confocal microscope therefore merges some synapses and gives a count that is too low. The overlap between LgLG1a and LgLG1b gives no reason to expand the sample. The experiment has one spGFP pair. The two groups are therefore different animals, and they never appear in one image.

5.7 Pilot and stop rule

This document defines the rule below. No publication gives this rule as a criterion.

  • Run the line that carries the spGFP1-10 half alone, group A, group B and group C. Use a small number of animals. Use one session, one stain and one set of imaging settings.
  • Read the line that carries the spGFP1-10 half alone first.
  • Read group C. Record whether group C saturates.
  • Read group A and group B. Use the scale that group C sets: counts or an intensity.

1 · Chromosome constraint

Chromosome 3 is full in this design. The two halves of the t-GRASP pair both sit on chromosome 3. Every other transgene in this design must therefore sit on chromosome 2 or on chromosome X.

Chromosome 2 carries the sensory driver line and the target driver line. Chromosome 3 carries the two GRASP halves and nothing else. Read the rest of this document against that rule. A stock fails the rule in two ways. The stock puts a transgene on chromosome 3 that is not a GRASP half. The stock puts a transgene at attP2, and a GRASP half already holds attP2.

The GRASP reagents on chromosome 3 cause no problem in this design. The GRASP reagents on chromosome 2 collide with the two driver lines. Chromosome 2 already carries the sensory driver line and the target driver line. State this point before you plan a cross.

3 · Why only one target

The experiment has one target neuron. This limit comes from the stocks that the laboratory holds. The steps below give the reason.

  • ppk25 exists only as GAL4, BDSC 93028. The ppk23 arm must therefore also use GAL4, BDSC 93026. The two arms then compare. The laboratory holds a ppk23-LexA line, and that line has no ppk25 partner.
  • The driver line for the target neuron must therefore use LexA. Two GAL4 lines in one animal label both sets of cells together.
  • R56C09-lexA, BDSC 53584, is the only target driver line in the laboratory that uses LexA. It drives PPN1.
  • SS103018 and the four vAB3 candidate lines are split-GAL4 lines. A split-GAL4 target line takes the GAL4 system. No system then remains for the sensory driver line.

The panel therefore has one point, PPN1, until a second reagent arrives. The other documents in this set list further target neurons and rank them. The current stocks do not reach those target neurons. A second point needs ppk25 in a second binary expression system. A LexA driver line for a second target neuron also gives a second point.

4 · Stocks to clean

4.1 ppk23-LexA

The stock is ;ppk23-LexA/CyO; 22B10-Gal4/TM3, Ser. The 22B10-GAL4 sits on chromosome 3. You must remove it for two reasons. Chromosome 3 must stay free. A stray GAL4 also drives the UAS half of the GRASP pair.

  • Cross the stock to a stock that carries two balancers.
  • Select the progeny that carry Ser. These flies carry TM3, Ser and no 22B10-GAL4.
  • Select the same flies for CyO without Sp. These flies carry ppk23-LexA.
  • Cross brothers to sisters. Keep the new stock. Keep the first stock unchanged.

Ser is dominant. You can therefore do this cleanup by eye. It needs no PCR.

4.2 ppk25-GAL4

BDSC 93028 carries one insert on chromosome 2 and one insert on chromosome 3. Remove the insert on chromosome 3. You have two reasons for this step. The first reason is the chromosome rule in section 1. The second reason is the insert count. Two inserts give a stronger driver line than one insert. ppk23-GAL4 has one insert. The experiment compares the two driver lines against each other.

Warning. The two inserts carry the same marker. You cannot follow them by eye. You must use PCR to show that the insert on chromosome 3 is gone. Plan the primers and the genomic DNA preparation before you set up the cross.

The insert count is one difference between the two driver lines. The two stocks also belong to different construct classes. Nobody checked whether every ppk25 positive cell is also a ppk23 positive cell. A matched insert count removes one known difference. It leaves the difference in construct class.

5 · The elav line

The design uses a third group of animals. In that group, elav-GAL4 replaces the ppk driver line. That group gives the total input to the target neuron. It also makes the numbers comparable between target neurons. It needs no second colour and no second reporter.

  • Check the chromosome of the elav-GAL4 line in the laboratory. This document does not record it. The line collides with the GRASP halves if it sits on chromosome 3. You then need another elav line.
  • Test whether elav-GAL4 labels the target neuron. It does label the target neuron. PPN1 carries the post half in this design. The neuron can therefore make a signal with itself.

Warning. The denominator group saturates. PPN1 takes 33476 input synapses. That much signal on one arbour gives one continuous area of fluorescence. You cannot count separate puncta in it. If group C saturates, read all three groups as intensity. Decide this point before you image.

6 · Absent from stock centres

A stock centre holds only a part of the lines that laboratories build. The ppk23-LexA line in this laboratory shows this. FlyBase carries FBtp0158874, M{ppk23-lexA::p65.V}. That entry is a 2.6 kb ppk23 promoter that drives lexA::p65, from Vernier et al. 2023. FlyBase shows 0 stocks for it. The laboratory holds the reagent. It may be the same reagent as the Dickson stock GJ1472. Compare the Janelia identifier DG78 against it.

Always ask other laboratories before you build a new construct. A build takes months. An email takes a day.

The missing ppk25-LexA line blocks the second point of the panel. No stock centre lists such a line. FlyBase holds no ppk25 record in LexA, QF or split-GAL4 form. By the rule above, this absence is a reason to ask other laboratories. It does not show that no such line exists.

9 · What to order

9.1 Ask first

  • Ask other laboratories for a ppk25-LexA line before you build one. Start with the Dickson stocks. The laboratory already holds GJ1472 from that source. Ask Greg.
  • Ask whether GJ1472 and FBtp0158874 are the same construct. Compare the Janelia identifier DG78.
  • Ask about an Ir52b driver line outside attP2, and about an Ir52b-LexA line. No source lists either line.
  • Ask Joshua Lillvis, now at Texas A&M, about the current expansion chemistry. Ask him about the BRP counting configuration. Mojtaba Tavakoli and Paul Tillberg at Janelia work on the same project. Barry Dickson was last author on the 2022 paper. The same collaboration holds the fly stocks. All these people belong to one group.

9.2 Build

  • Build a ppk25-LexA line. This single reagent extends the panel from one point to two points. The GRASP halves occupy attP2 and chromosome 3. Do not put the new line at attP2. Do not put it on chromosome 3. Put it on chromosome 2 or on chromosome X.
  • If you build the line, match the construct class of ppk23-LexA as closely as you can. Give the new line one insert. The two arms then differ only in the receptor type.

9.3 Order

  • Order mouse anti-GFP, Sigma G6539, at 1:100. Macpherson et al. 2015 use it as the anti-GRASP antibody. Its specificity rests on established practice, and nobody published proof of it. Test the antibody yourself.
  • Order chicken anti-GFP, Abcam ab13970, as the counter-stain. The same paper reports that this antibody prefers the free spGFP1-10 half. The antibody therefore shows the expression pattern.
  • Gel chemistry destroys cyanine dyes. Do not order Cy3, Cy5 or Alexa Fluor 647 for any protocol that includes a gel. Order Alexa Fluor 488, CF568 or Atto 647N as the secondary dyes.
  • Make a control line that carries the spGFP1-10 half alone and no partner. This line must stay blank. Make it at the same time as the experimental crosses.
  • Order the STaR components if you take the BRP counting route. That route uses the two binary expression systems that the laboratory already holds. The ppk-GAL4 line drives the BRP tag. R56C09-lexA marks the target neuron membrane. The other documents in this set give the method and the expected synapse counts.

9.4 Missing information

  • Apply MCFO to the four vAB3 candidate split-GAL4 lines. Nobody knows which AN09B017 member each line labels. The members of that group sit at opposite ends of the target list. A line that labels the whole group gives an average value near the middle of the range. A reader can mistake that value for a real measurement of no difference. Such a line therefore gives a misleading result. This job needs imaging only, on stocks that the laboratory already holds.
  • Record the chromosome of the elav-GAL4 line, as in section 5.
  • Record the insert count of the cleaned ppk25-GAL4 stock, with the PCR result, next to the stock.
